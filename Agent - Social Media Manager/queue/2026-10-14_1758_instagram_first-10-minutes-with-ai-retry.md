# Caption

You've heard everyone is using AI. Maybe you've even made an account. Now you're staring at an empty box, wondering what a "good" question looks like and whether you're about to do something dumb.

You're not. Nothing breaks, and it costs nothing. Only 34% of workers say their employer offers any AI training, so most of us are teaching ourselves. Here's the shortest version I know.

1. Pick one real task. Something from your week you mildly dislike, like a tricky email or a meeting summary. Not "learn AI." A task.

2. Open a free tool. Claude, ChatGPT and Gemini all have free plans. Cost is the barrier 57% of people name, so stay on free until you have a reason to leave.

3. Strip out anything confidential. This is the step people skip. What you type goes to a company's server, so swap names for "Client A", round the numbers, and leave out anything secret, like passwords or contracts. If your work has an AI policy, follow it.

4. Give it a situation, then a request. "I work in finance. Here's a draft email to my manager about a budget overrun: [paste, details changed]. Make it shorter and calmer, and keep the numbers." Situation first, request second. That's the whole formula.

5. Push back. "Too formal." "Put the number first." The first answer is a draft, and steering it is the skill.

6. Check it. AI writes confident sentences, wrong ones included. Read every number against your source, then put the real names and figures back in yourself.

In Part 2 I'll set up VS Code and GitHub step by step, so your work stops disappearing into a chat window.

Cheers!
