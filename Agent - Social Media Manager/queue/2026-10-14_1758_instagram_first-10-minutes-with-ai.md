# Caption

You've heard that everyone is using AI. You've maybe made an account. And now you're looking at an empty box, wondering what a "good" question even looks like, and whether you're about to do something dumb.

You're not. There's nothing to break here, and it costs nothing. Only 34% of workers say their employer offers any AI training, so most people are teaching themselves. This is the shortest version of that I know.

**Step 1: Pick one real task.**
Something from your actual week that you mildly dislike. A tricky email, a meeting summary, a formula you can never remember. Don't pick "learn AI." Pick a task.

**Step 2: Open a free tool.**
Claude, ChatGPT and Gemini all have free plans. Make an account with your email. Cost is the barrier 57% of people name, so start on the free plan and stay there until you have a reason to leave it.

**Step 3: Strip out anything confidential.**
This is the step people skip, so it comes before your first prompt. A free tool is a company's server, and what you type goes there. Depending on the tool and the plan, chats may be reviewed or used to improve the model. Defaults differ, so open the privacy or data settings in your account and read what yours does.

Then build one habit. Before you paste anything, swap client and colleague names for placeholders ("Client A"), round the numbers, and delete whatever identifies a deal, a person or a price. Keep the shape of the problem and lose the details that point to a real one.

Some things never go in at all: passwords, contracts, anything your company has told you to keep in-house. If your employer has an AI policy, follow it. If you're not sure, ask, or use a made-up example. Worry about privacy is one of the top reasons people hold back from AI, and it's a fair worry. This step is how you answer it.

**Step 4: Give it a situation, then a request.**
An empty prompt gets you a generic answer. Try this one, with your own details changed:

*"I work in finance. Here's a draft email to my manager about a budget overrun on Project X: [paste, with names and exact figures changed]. Make it shorter and calmer, and keep the numbers."*

Your situation first, your request second. That's the whole formula.

**Step 5: Push back.**
Say "too formal." Say "put the number first." Say "give me a version for someone who's annoyed." The first answer is a draft, and the skill you're building is steering it.

**Step 6: Check it.**
AI writes confident sentences, including wrong ones. Read every number and every claim against your source before you send anything. Then put your real names and figures back in yourself. This takes two minutes and it keeps you out of trouble.
